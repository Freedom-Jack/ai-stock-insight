# Stock Insight decision memo

November 5, 2026 · For the lead AI architect

**Pursue a personal research desk for the companies you follow.** Give it a filing, a selected email, a Stockwatch alert, or a question. It should read the relevant material, compare it with what you last reviewed, and return a brief you can act on in one sitting. Its most valuable output may be a single passage worth reading and a reason to skip the other five. You decide what the evidence means for your investment.

**What that buys you.** After the Taiwan earthquake, TSMC estimated roughly NT$3 billion in losses after insurance. Its Q2 accounts booked roughly that amount. A summary could repeat the number twice. The desk should catch the change from *estimated* to *recognized*, close the accounting question, and keep the separate question about NVIDIA shipments open. [TSMC’s April assessment](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617924000050/a20240418.htm) · [TSMC’s Q2 accounts, note 34](https://investor.tsmc.com/sites/ir/financial-report/2024/TSMC%202024Q2%20Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements_E.pdf#page=51)

**Borrow the agent runtime.** Preserve Stockwatch’s dated evidence. Test whether owning the history of individual claims, their origins, and your open questions saves enough repeated work to justify custom software. Source-linked filing chat, saved research prompts and thesis trackers already exist. [Quartr](https://quartr.com/features/ai-chat) · [Anthropic’s equity research workflows](https://github.com/anthropics/financial-services)

**Decision for review.** Test the idea on paper against your current routine and a well-prompted research assistant with a saved note. Judge them on the *second* disclosure, including time spent verifying and correcting the answer. If the saved note works as well, keep it. If the desk wins, test whether an agent can reproduce the gain on unfamiliar material before committing to software.
