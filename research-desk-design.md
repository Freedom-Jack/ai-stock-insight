# Stock Insight as a personal research desk

November 5, 2026 · Design proposal · [Decision memo](decision-memo.md)

## Returning to a question after an alert

Imagine you follow NVIDIA. On April 5 you read Stockwatch’s Taiwan earthquake update and marked one question for later: *Could disruption at a supplier delay NVIDIA shipments?* On the evening of April 18 you open the same question. The following is a proposed interaction reconstructed from public records. The user actions and email are illustrative.

> **TSMC has put a cost on the earthquake. The open NVIDIA question is about deliveries.**
>
> **Since your April 5 review** — TSMC previously reported that most equipment had recovered, with exceptions on some lines. It now says tools were back by the end of day three, but wafers already in process had to be scrapped. Management expects little effect on its Q2 revenue and about half a percentage point of lost Q2 gross margin. Recovery of equipment and a cost to the quarter can both be true.
>
> **What I would read** — The final three paragraphs of TSMC’s April 18 statement give its revenue outlook, margin estimate and loss after insurance. The two press links in your queue carry the same Reuters dispatch. Reuters attributes the margin figure to TSMC’s Taiwan exchange filing. For that figure, all three links take you back to management.
>
> **What could change the NVIDIA reading** — Look for a customer or supplier identifying an affected product, order or delivery window. NVIDIA’s filing establishes that it uses TSMC and also names Samsung. The disclosed supplier relationship leaves order allocation unresolved.

[TSMC’s April 5 statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617924000038/a20240405.htm) · [April 18 assessment, final paragraphs](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617924000050/a20240418.htm) · [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/TSMC-TAIWAN-SEMICONDUCTOR-6492349/news/TSMC-estimates-losses-of-92-4-mln-due-to-Taiwan-earthquake-46470240/) · [Same dispatch via Radio USA](https://radiousa.com/2024/04/18/tsmc-estimates-losses-of-92-4-million-due-to-taiwan-earthquake/) · [NVIDIA FY2024 10-K, Manufacturing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000029/nvda-20240128.htm)

You select **“What else could I be missing?”** The agent highlights another sentence in NVIDIA’s filing. The company buys memory from Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung. Micron disclosed that it was still recovering and estimated the effect on its own DRAM supply at up to a mid-single-digit percentage of a calendar quarter’s output. There are now two routes to investigate, wafer fabrication and memory. Micron measured a different operation. How it allocated supplies to NVIDIA is still open. You pin the memory question for the next NVIDIA review. [NVIDIA’s named suppliers](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000029/nvda-20240128.htm) · [Micron 8-K, Item 7.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000110465924045952/tm2411566d1_8k.htm)

You add an analyst email that cites the Reuters story and says channel checks suggest delayed GPU shipments. The desk folds the dollar estimate under TSMC. It keeps the shipment claim visible under the analyst’s name, labels the underlying contacts unknown, and asks which product, customer and promised date the checks cover. Trust in the analyst can move the email up your reading list. It cannot turn unnamed contacts into independent confirmation. You can mark the question *worth asking* without accepting the claim.

**At the next accounts review**, ask “What actually changed?” The desk opens note 34 of the two financial statements side by side. The figures round to the same amount. The change is in the verb and in where the loss appeared.

| Earlier evidence | Later evidence | What you can settle |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 accounts, subsequent-events note. TSMC estimated about NT$3bn of earthquake losses, net of insurance, for recognition in Q2. | Q2 accounts, disaster-loss note. TSMC recognized about NT$3bn net, primarily in cost of revenue and other operating income and expenses. | Close “Did TSMC book the anticipated loss?” Keep any customer-delivery question separate. The booked total spans income-statement lines, so it cannot by itself verify the precise gross-margin effect. |

**A quiet-day use.** Drop in Amazon’s 2024 annual filing and ask, “When I compare AWS profitability, which server-life assumption am I carrying forward?” Its June 10-Q described extending server lives from five to six years. The 10-K subsequently shortened the lives of a *subset* of servers and networking equipment from six to five, effective January 2025. For affected assets on hand at year-end, Amazon anticipated about $0.7bn less 2025 operating income and separately identified about $0.6bn from accelerated depreciation on early retirements. The changes primarily affect AWS. Amazon gives the $0.7bn at company level. The desk would open Note 1 and flag the subset and segment qualifications before you adjust an AWS margin comparison. This job can start with two documents and zero alerts. ## What the investor sees and controls

Start with one investor following a few companies. Opening a document, forwarding selected text, asking a question and following a Stockwatch link all lead to the same place. If you only supply a document, the desk proposes a question and starts the comparison. Setup requires neither a portfolio import nor an investment thesis. You can specify a prior review, or let it select the last comparable disclosure and show you which one it used.

The home view is a short reading queue. First come corrections to evidence you relied on and changes that bear on a question with an approaching review date. Each entry explains its place in a sentence, such as “You asked about Q2 costs. The filed figure is available.” The rest go into *waiting for evidence* or *review when convenient*. A quiet session can end with “The three new items repeat the estimate you reviewed.” Reserve a small discovery slot for a well-evidenced connection outside your saved questions. This guards against a diligent assistant only finding things you already thought to ask.

The first screen gives a proposed reading decision, the change since your last review, the strongest alternative that fits the evidence, and a passage worth opening. Expand any claim to see its source, earlier wording, who made it and what is still inferred. Select *show sources first* if you want to form a view before seeing the synthesis. You can split a mixed claim, correct a source relationship, ask for contrary evidence, mark an item reviewed or close a question. Your note stays separately attributed. Opening a link alone never records agreement.

Keep one history per question, with links to the relevant companies and incidents. Two questions about the same quake can close on different dates. A quarterly review can draw on several incidents. The next session resumes from what **you** last reviewed and distinguishes research added in the meantime. By default, new analysis waits in the desk. Existing urgent alerts keep their own delivery path.

## The research record

The portable unit is a **claim about a named subject, in a defined period, linked to the evidence for it**. Store the issuer or speaker separately from the publisher, and the document separately from the passage. A release on an exchange and its SEC copy can share an origin. A single newspaper story can repeat management on one point and contribute original reporting on another. Agreement alone is weak evidence of copying. Work on source dependence in structured data motivates this distinction. Applying it to news and private email still requires testing. [Dong, Berti-Équille and Srivastava on source dependence](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol2/vldb09-pvldb47.pdf)

| Record | Minimum content and purpose |
|---|---|
| Question and review | User’s wording, scope, last reviewed revision, relevant entities, deadline or reopening condition, and separately authored user notes. |
| Source version and passage | Stable document identity, version or content hash, publisher and original speaker, event and reporting dates, publication and first receipt, language, access/retention rules, exact page/section/table cell and surrounding qualifiers. Mark an unknown clock or incomplete extract. |
| Claim and origin | Entity, proposition, period, units and basis, speaker, observation/estimate/interpretation, supporting or contrary passages. Connect citations, syndicated copies, translations, amendments and calculations. Label origin **documented**, **suspected** or **unknown**, with a reason. A similarity match proposes a link for review. |
| Interpretation and revision | Which claims support it, assumptions needed, best contrary evidence, what observation would distinguish the alternatives, method version and as-of cutoff. A later revision records what changed, why, which prior briefs relied on it and whether you saw them. |

For a filing comparison, align the company, topic, period, reporting scope and accounting basis before comparing values. Preserve the table heading, unit, denominator and footnote. Handle reclassifications, amendments and withdrawn language explicitly. A missing passage is a reason to inspect the full document before declaring a withdrawal. Standardized, entity-wide XBRL facts can verify some numbers. Guidance, custom metrics and segment details often require the original filing. Keep calculation inputs and units beside the result so another person can reproduce it. [SEC description of EDGAR and Company Facts coverage](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)

When two passages conflict, first test for different times, scopes or definitions. If the conflict survives, show both and explain which source was in a position to know what. Give the stronger reading when the evidence warrants one. A speculative alternative earns space only if it could change the reading decision and there is a way to examine it. A finding can also be “The two available sources cannot answer that question.” Record the missing observation and stop.

## What the agent does

Use one bounded investigator with read-only tools. The user delegates a result, so the investigator can choose the next source, revise a hypothesis or finish early. A short series of explicit steps makes those choices inspectable.

1. **Frame the assignment.** Select the question, review cutoff, last comparable document, permitted sources and a reading deadline. Check whether the question already has an answer. For an unsolicited document, attach only to questions with a plausible common subject and period.
2. **Read for change.** Retrieve the current and prior passages, including nearby footnotes. Extract scoped claims with passage IDs. Follow attribution back to the speaker. Search beyond the initial packet when an original, comparable release or a direct observer could settle a material gap.
3. **Reason and challenge.** Write the best-supported explanation, name the assumption that matters most, and seek the observation most likely to overturn it. A parallel reader can examine a genuinely separate source or an original-language qualification. Compare its evidence with the first reader’s. Two model outputs tracing to one passage add one origin.
4. **Verify and publish a draft.** Resolve each material citation to its stored version, check the quoted span and units, and recompute derived values. A second reading checks whether the passage actually supports the claim, including its scope. A mechanical match alone cannot establish that. Drop or label unsupported claims. Return the short brief with the missing evidence, even when a provider times out.
5. **Revisit on a material change.** A new source revision finds dependent claims and questions. Invalidate the affected conclusions, show a correction to anyone who reviewed them, and preserve the old as-of view. Repeated delivery of the same version adds no new brief. An open question can wait as a record for months and start a fresh run when evidence arrives.

As initial limits to test, give routine work one comparison pair, one contrary-evidence search and up to five retrieved documents or five minutes. Stop sooner when another source would repeat the same origin, the discriminator is undisclosed, or the remaining uncertainty cannot change what to read. Flag any high-value lead left outside the limit. Going deeper is a user choice with a fresh scope and allowance. All figures here are proposed limits.

### How to use an analyst you trust

Offer two distinct actions. **“What did this analyst actually argue?”** retrieves their dated, permitted words, identifies their basis, and shows where they agree or differ with other sources. **“Use this method on my question”** applies a user-approved checklist and labels the result as the desk’s analysis. The latter remains available when the analyst has never commented on the company.

For example, adapt Aswath Damodaran’s published practice of translating a narrative into business drivers. A user-approved method might ask which of units, timing, price, or cost must change for a supplier disruption to affect the quarter. In the TSMC case, it would separate lost wafers from deferred shipments, find the issuer’s estimate for margin, and leave customer revenue blank. A second method, matching estimates to subsequent accounts, would send you straight to note 34. Neither output purports to be Damodaran’s opinion on the stock. [Damodaran, *Numbers and Narrative*](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/eqnotes/narrative%26numbers.pdf)

Let the investor select an authorized example or write their own rules, review the proposed checklist, and save a version with applicability, required evidence and a stopping condition. Test it on a held-out case and a case where it should abstain. For an analyst’s new note, record whether they added a firsthand observation, revised an assumption or offered a different interpretation of shared evidence. Authority may change which question to pursue. It should never silently raise the confidence attached to an issuer’s number.

## What to build or borrow

**For the comparison, borrow the whole research environment.** Assemble documents and a saved question in an existing assistant. Give it a competent reusable instruction to compare passages, trace origins, challenge the leading reading, and update the prior note. Quartr documents exact-page links and reusable prompts. AlphaSense documents custom scheduled agents. Anthropic publishes equity research workflows including earnings analysis and thesis tracking. These are credible alternatives. Their documentation establishes feature overlap. Performance on your recurring questions is an open empirical question. [Quartr AI chat](https://quartr.com/features/ai-chat) · [AlphaSense custom workflow agents](https://help.alpha-sense.com/hc/en-us/articles/48564685232275-Custom-and-Scheduled-Workflow-Agents) · [Anthropic financial services workflows](https://github.com/anthropics/financial-services)

**If the evidence earns a build**, own the part that took repeated manual work. Candidates are the private question history, claim-to-origin links, filing comparison rules and corrections. Keep the evaluation examples portable. Borrow the agent loop, document parser and permitted search adapters. The Claude Agent SDK, for example, can expose custom tools. One investigator equipped to find a prior filing, open a passage, trace an attribution and request a verified calculation would be enough for the first comparison. A separate application step validates and commits its draft. [Agent SDK custom tools](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/custom-tools)

A local relational store and one short-running worker fit the single-investor assumption. Commit a complete brief revision and its evidence references together, keyed by question, input versions, cutoff and method/run version. A retry with the same run key returns the existing result. Pin the inputs for each run, check that the case revision still matches at commit, and mark a stale run for reconciliation. Preserve the actual model result and tool receipts. A deliberate rerun becomes a new revision, so auditing last month’s brief never depends on reproducing a stochastic answer.

Use a borrowed checkpointed orchestrator such as LangGraph **if** repeated mid-run interruptions, fan-out or human handoffs make restarting expensive. Keep its run state separate from the long-lived question and evidence records. If a managed research service can supply exportable, versioned passage references and correction behavior at lower total effort, keep the presentation and method there too. TradingAgents illustrates another possible design, where specialist debate feeds a trading decision. A dissenting search assignment is useful here. Voting, targets and trade execution would optimize a different outcome. [LangGraph persistence](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/persistence) · [TradingAgents research and workflow](https://tauric.ai/research/tradingagents)

**Access is a design constraint.** Start with public filings, direct issuer disclosures and deliberately selected material. For each source, track permission to fetch, store, send to a processor, retain excerpts and display results. An email subscription alone settles none of those downstream choices. Keep private mail, notes and any holdings local by default. Allow link-only or user-authored notes where text processing rights are unclear. For example, Quartr distinguishes personal subscriber MCP use from product integration through its API. Give an external search tool a public issuer and a public question only. Treat document text and embedded instructions as evidence, with no authority to change tools or send messages. Deleting private material should remove its extracts and invalidate derived passages or briefs that depended on it. [Quartr integration terms of use in its MCP documentation](https://mcp.quartr.com/docs) · [Research on indirect prompt injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12173)

## How the recommendation could lose

**After design review**, start on paper with two or three companies you actually follow, one recurring question and one analyst or method you choose. Use existing access. Begin with a few sequential historical packets containing a copy, a genuine independent observation, a correction and an unanswerable question. Hold later evidence back until the second review. These cases test mechanics. A model or a person can remember famous outcomes, so they offer little evidence about live usefulness.

Then adapt the monitoring paper’s eight-week comparison to **two or three real reading decisions per week**, including quiet days and items you would ordinarily skip. Compare your usual routine, an existing assistant with a strong prompt and saved note, and the proposed desk format assembled by hand. On controlled cases, use the same permitted material, cutoff, previous note and time allowance. Separately log discoveries made with each tool’s normal source access. That separates better analysis from better entitlements. [Monitoring research, prospective test](monitoring-research.md#testing-the-value-of-analysis-separately)

Register the question and what qualifies as material before reading. If working solo, assign each fresh live episode to one approach, rotating across comparable assignments. Time only the first reading. Check passages, omissions and repeats afterward. A second reader, if available and entitled to the material, can prepare or grade historical packets without seeing which tool produced an answer. Track preparation, investor review and corrections separately. Require at least one question with two successive live disclosures before judging memory, even if that takes longer than eight weeks.

| Uncertainty | Evidence and proposed decision rule |
|---|---|
| Does the desk return more attention than it consumes? | Include preparation, reading, verification, corrections and ongoing upkeep. Apply measured time per comparable case to your observed weekly volume. Look for **30 net minutes/week** saved against the best available alternative, or an additional verified material finding in **two separate reviews** that changes what you investigate. Both routes require comparable coverage and no increase in consequential errors. Show individual cases and times alongside any average. |
| Can you safely skip the repeated coverage? | Audit every brief’s material claims against the opened passages. Separately count omitted material changes, false merges of independent observations, false claims of independence, and unresolved origins presented honestly. Check randomly sampled suppressed items and silent days. A fabricated source, consequential unsupported inference, look-ahead fact or missed correction stops that method pending review. Abstaining on everything loses on coverage. [Liu, Zhang and Liang on citation precision and recall](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.467/) |
| Does memory or a trusted method earn the extra machinery? | On second disclosures, compare the time to find what changed and correct every dependent reading. On some cases, hide the named method or omit it. Record whether it finds a new supported driver or changes the next document opened. If a saved note and prompt tie, retain those. If only side-by-side filing comparisons win, narrow the product to that job. |
| Is the bottleneck access or reasoning? | Record missing originals, unusable licenses, unavailable customer detail and time lost obtaining sources. If private material is essential and cannot enter the proposed runtime, keep analysis local or stay within an entitled existing tool. If broader sources explain the gain, evaluate an appropriately licensed reader before engineering an agent. |
| Does the assistant shape the conclusion too much? | On some cases ask for your first question before showing synthesis. Check if you can state the evidence against the leading explanation and change or dismiss it. If fluent briefs suppress a warranted dissent, default to passages first. Experiments outside investing found that requiring active consideration could reduce overreliance on an AI suggestion, with a cost in perceived ease. Test that tradeoff here. [Buçinca and colleagues](https://iis.seas.harvard.edu/papers/2021/bucinca21trust.pdf) |

Track cash per accepted brief across source entitlements, retrieval, model calls, retries and retention. The monitoring paper’s roughly $5/month infrastructure scenario covers light monitoring compute and storage. Research needs its own estimate. Compare any proposed paid increment with verified net hours saved per month multiplied by what those hours are worth to you.

**Review decision.** Compare one recurring question through two successive disclosures. The first answer tests reading. The second tests whether the desk remembers what mattered and updates it for a reason you can verify. A paper win justifies a separate feasibility test with a borrowed agent on unfamiliar, frozen packets. Require it to preserve material qualifiers, handle unknown origins and corrections, and still save time after verification. If it passes, own only the capability responsible for the gain. A saved research practice or a specialized filing reader are both good outcomes.
