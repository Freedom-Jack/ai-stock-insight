# Personal monitoring and early warning for a US equity investor

Research and design recommendation · November 5, 2026

**Keep native alerts for company news. Pilot on-demand investment research alongside alerts that explain how overseas developments affect a US company.** Both benefit from the same dated evidence and reproducible calculations. Either can justify itself independently. A Taiwan power bulletin can prompt a supplier review before the chip company comments. Evidence of recovery can later close the warning.

The proposed pilot follows three US chip companies, one overseas supplier and a broad-market benchmark. It starts with existing tools and a spreadsheet. Holdings are optional. If automation earns a place, a managed service would cost about $5/month for computing and storage at the modeled workload, plus any research, delivery and data charges. Run an eight-week comparison and retain the capabilities that improve warning lead, answer quality or total research time.

The first decisions worth supporting are *what deserves research before my next review*, *which holdings share this exposure*, and *what evidence would change my investment thesis*. These are useful even when doing nothing is the right response. A known date can buy preparation time. A source near the event can buy time before an issuer confirms an effect. Some events first become public when the company announces them. There is no defensible promise of advance notice for those.

## Which warnings deserve a place

These priorities assume one individual tracking US-listed equities, including foreign issuers where relevant, with minutes-to-hours awareness and days-to-weeks preparation. The pilot uses a small illustrative watchlist, immediate phone alerts for genuinely urgent developments, a daily premarket email and a short weekly discovery review. Spending and hardware are open choices. Compare each paid increment with the time or warning lead it buys. The examples do not assume any holdings, account access, position sizes or trading strategy.

| Priority | Decision and earliest useful evidence | What limits its value |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Material changes at a company | Revisit a thesis when an issuer changes guidance, loses a named customer, discloses an operating interruption or quantifies a new obligation. Compare the new statement with the last dated one. | Highly verifiable and personal, often with zero warning of the announcement itself. The broker or issuer may arrive first. Copying its headline earns little. |
| 2. Scheduled events and overlapping exposures | Prepare questions ahead of earnings, supplier sales, regulatory effective dates, elections and major macro releases. Bundle events that affect the same names. | A calendar does most of this alone. Incremental value comes from a changed date, an unexpected overlap, or new evidence about the range of outcomes. |
| 3. Overseas operations and policy | Follow one disclosed supplier, facility, product or market one step upstream. A local authority or operator can open a watch hours or days before the US issuer comments, subject to evidence and delivery. | Best custom experiment. Relationships go stale, the affected plant may serve a different product, and a license requirement may have little financial effect. Direction and magnitude frequently remain unknown. |
| 4. Sector-wide bottlenecks and slow changes | Track, for example, a dated fuel-cost sensitivity alongside carrier diversions and fuel-market evidence. Look for several holdings dependent on the same input. Consider a debt maturity or permit deadline if stock selection makes it more relevant. | Higher maintenance. A container booking restriction is weak evidence about jet-fuel prices. A maturity date alone says little about refinancing difficulty. Start in the digest and require more direct evidence to escalate. |
| 5. Open-ended global discovery | Reserve one slot in the weekly brief for a credible development outside the known exposure list. Explain who could be affected and which filing or local source would verify it. | Media coverage is uneven. Count useful discoveries per hour reviewed. Popularity, translated copies and social chatter can all grow without the underlying risk changing. |

There is a reason to test the supplier idea and a reason to be cautious. Cohen and Frazzini found historical return predictability across disclosed customer–supplier links. That was a particular sample and strategy. McLean and Pontiff found substantial post-publication deterioration across a wider set of published return predictors. A separate market microstructure study measured reactions to scheduled macro surprises in SPY and E-mini futures within milliseconds. A person who understands an exposure before breakfast can be better prepared even after prices have moved. Evaluate that benefit directly. [Customer–supplier study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x) · [Out-of-sample and post-publication study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365) · [Market response to macro releases](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/31/12/4650/4956248)

## What analysis is worth doing

A good test on a quiet day is whether you would open the result yourself. A comparison of two filings, a calculation you can reproduce, or a map of several holdings’ dependence on one supplier can justify that attention. Each can also improve the *next* alert. A bankruptcy filing or an imminent deadline already has enough meaning to notify you promptly. Requiring a research report first would reduce its value.

| Question | Data and method | What makes the answer trustworthy |
|---|---|---|
| What changed in the investment thesis? | Align the same issuer’s current and prior disclosures by section, reporting period and metric definition. Show the old and new passages together, identify withdrawals and record what would contradict management’s explanation. Produce a short pre-earnings reading list even without an alert. | Check both passages in the original filing. A restatement, changed segment, acquisition or switch from GAAP to a management-adjusted measure can invalidate a numerical comparison. SEC Company Facts covers standardized facts applying to the whole entity. Use the original tables and footnotes for segment data, custom metrics and guidance. Historical research found useful information in filing changes and also found that regulation drove substantial growth in disclosure text. Use a text difference to direct review. [SEC data scope](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) · [Study of changes in filings](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/576a041c-19c4-47d8-b28d-2a5ef079f54f) · [Study of disclosure growth](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8429/) |
| What can be established before the next announcement? | Reconcile monthly releases to a quarterly figure, or compare progress with a dated guidance range. Keep currencies, units and fiscal periods explicit. Distinguish the issuer’s guidance from any licensed, time-stamped analyst consensus. | Recompute with a calculator and resolve against the eventual filing. If a conversion rate, definition or consensus vintage is missing, show the sensitivity or leave the comparison open. The TSMC example below can be reproduced in a spreadsheet. |
| Which businesses or holdings share a vulnerability? | Trace a named input, region, supplier or customer to the specific issuer disclosure. Look for alternatives such as a second supplier or offsetting revenue. With an optional positions snapshot, separately calculate capital invested in the linked businesses. | Preserve the evidence for each connection and when it was last checked. An issuer can name a supplier while leaving its purchasing share undisclosed. Grouping linked positions measures *how much capital merits review*. The share of production exposed, probability of disruption and resulting loss are separate questions. |
| How consequential could a development be? | Use management’s disclosed sensitivities or explicit hypothetical shocks. Delta estimated that each sustained 1¢/gallon increase in jet fuel would add about $40 million in annual fuel expense at roughly four billion gallons. A 10¢ scenario at that volume implies roughly $400 million of additional annual fuel expense before offsets. | Reconcile units and horizon, and inspect fuel volume, fares and refinery offsets. A port advisory alone supplies no 10¢ fuel-price forecast. Nor is the expense change a net earnings or share-price estimate. Define a dated sign of escalation and a sign of recovery. [Delta 10-K, Item 7A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27904/000002790426000013/dal-20251231.htm) |
| What remains worth investigating? | Maintain a short list of open questions for the next supplier release, earnings call or official decision. For each record the best competing explanation, the observation that would distinguish the two, and a review date. | Revisit expired questions, record reversals, and count answers that actually changed a review. For price attribution, coincident releases and changing expectations often leave multiple explanations. Use date-matched benchmark returns to describe relative performance and reserve causal language for cases with identifying evidence. |

### A quarter that could be reconstructed before earnings

**Facts available July 10, 2025.** TSMC reported first-half sales of NT$1,773,046 million. Its April release had put first-quarter sales at NT$839,254 million. In April the company had guided second-quarter revenue to US$28.4–29.2 billion and shown an exchange-rate assumption of NT$32.5 per US dollar. [First-half sales](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617925000080/tsm-revenue20250710.htm) · [First-quarter sales](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617925000033/tsm-revenue20250410.htm) · [Original guidance](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2025/q1)

**Calculation.** Subtracting the two comparable cumulative figures gives NT$933.792 billion of second-quarter sales. At 32.5, that converts to about US$28.73 billion. The conversion rate that puts the total at the *upper* end of guidance is about 31.98 (933.792 ÷ 29.2). **Interpretation.** An investor could already see quarterly sales in local currency. Comparing those sales with US-dollar guidance still required a defensible conversion. A useful earnings preview would show that threshold and look for the realized exchange rate. Customer-specific demand and a share-price response remain open.

**Resolution, only available later.** On July 17 TSMC reported NT$933.79 billion, US$30.07 billion and an average rate of 31.05. The local-currency calculation reconciles to the published precision. Using the old exchange assumption as though it were the realized rate would have put the dollar figure inside guidance incorrectly. This historical exercise demonstrates a method. Lead over the investor’s own tools needs a prospective test. [TSMC second-quarter management report](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-07/1fec9df3de99e6e9f190e1eda7179449381ce6d8/2Q25ManagementReport.pdf)

### What position sizes would add

Start with manual portfolio weights or an optional local broker export. A reproducible snapshot needs its effective date, accounts included, base currency, instrument identifiers, cash, values or quantities and date-matched prices/FX, and the account total. Reconcile the total before displaying percentages. Keep raw positions private and version imports after trades. A watchlist can still support company research without these data. To calculate *historical personal performance*, obtain transactions or dated holdings, external cash flows, fees and distributions as well. A snapshot taken today cannot establish what the investor held last quarter. [Example of a broker positions export](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/trader-desktop-user-guide/positions)

**Illustrative account, real dated fund input.** Suppose a $100,000 long-only USD account at the November 4, 2026 close held $10,000 of NVDA, $40,000 of SPY and $50,000 cash. State Street showed NVIDIA at **7.30% of the fund** that day, versus 7.31% of the index. The fund weight gives $10,000 + $40,000 × 7.30% = **about $12,920, or 12.92% of the account**, tied to NVIDIA shares. The remaining $37,080 of SPY plus cash reconciles to $100,000 when added to that exposure. An assumed 20% decline in NVDA alone with all other underlying prices fixed and full pass-through to SPY would cost about **$2,584, or 2.58%**. One could also ask what isolated NVDA move would move this account 1%. The answer is about 7.74% under the same assumptions. Neither shock has an assigned probability. The sponsor’s page rolls forward, so save the permitted dated input at the time of a real assessment. [State Street SPY fund holdings](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/individual/etfs/state-street-spdr-sp-500-etf-trust-spy)

For a supplier incident assessed against that November snapshot, 12.92% is a reason to review the holding. Each historical alert requires its own contemporaneous snapshot. It can justify checking a fab update sooner while leaving NVIDIA’s affected wafer volume unknown. The source’s credibility and the time before the next decision stay the same. Personalization should promote relevant incidents while retaining market-wide and unanticipated developments for discovery. A small holding with an imminent binary decision can warrant more attention than a large holding with a distant, speculative risk.

For transparent funds, match dated sponsor holdings to the account close and replace each fund wrapper with its constituent contributions exactly once. ETFs relying on SEC Rule 6c-11 publish prior-close holdings before regular trading opens. Check coverage and reuse rights fund by fund. Public N-PORT bulk files update quarterly and can support a dated cross-check. Publish any unmapped weight, input ages and valuation dates. Incomplete accounts, stale fund data, opaque funds, shorts, options and leveraged exposures need explicit separate treatment. For the pilot, retain a qualitative alert and withhold unsupported numerical totals. If a private snapshot is offline, expired or missing, send the public alert on time and offer a private calculation later. [SEC ETF disclosure guide](https://www.sec.gov/investment/exchange-traded-funds-small-entity-compliance-guide) · [N-PORT coverage and cadence](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets)

A more ambitious question is how closely the holdings have tracked market or sector returns. With aligned total-return histories, benchmark or factor series, FX and a declared estimation window, rolling regressions can describe co-movement. Test stability out of sample against simple benchmark exposure, report sensitivity to the window, and save the data vintage. Current weights can define a hypothetical basket for this exercise. Only historical holdings or transactions support claims about the investor’s past basket. Defer this work until repeated user questions justify the data and maintenance. [Research factor data and historical archives](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)

Buy or reuse portfolio analytics first when ordinary allocation, overlap, attribution or benchmarking supplies the answer. IBKR offers a free standalone PortfolioAnalyst account with additional capabilities for brokerage clients. Quartr’s free mobile app already lets investors question issuer material and inspect sources. A local spreadsheet and original filings give an independent check and keep holdings off a vendor’s servers. A custom analysis earns a place for the connection across sources, the point-in-time evidence, or the research time it saves. A broker integration can wait until manual refreshes cause measured errors. [PortfolioAnalyst scope](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/portfolioanalyst/overview.php) · [Quartr research features](https://quartr.com/products/mobile-app)

## Sources and their limits

Use global sources **where the business has an exposure** and maintain a broader discovery channel for surprises. Track legal entities and local identifiers, source language, original timezone, place of operations, and the evidence date for each relationship. Sales geography, customer headquarters and factory location answer different questions. NVIDIA’s FY2026 filing, for example, estimates that 76% of its data-center revenue from Taiwan-headquartered customers was attributable to end customers in the US and Europe. An unqualified “Taiwan revenue” alert would point the investor toward the wrong risk. [NVIDIA FY2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

## How information becomes an alert

1. **Describe what you follow.** Begin with 3–5 names or a paper watchlist and one broad index proxy. Keep at most ten relationships such as “TSMC manufactures wafers for NVIDIA” with a filing, date, scope and review deadline. Distinguish direct operations, named counterparties and sector analogies. A broad ETF needs a dated holdings source before attributing a company weight. Use a watchlist flag if holdings are private. Revalidate relationships each reporting cycle.
2. **Capture the evidence as received.** A scheduled job saves source ID, original URL and language, issuer or origin, event time, published/updated time, local first-seen time in UTC, revision and content hash. Store only permitted payloads or excerpts. Separate a translated claim from its original, retain corrections and missing timestamps, and give each incoming item an ID. Repeated identical items, syndicated copies, wire translations and SEC exhibits of the same release share a provenance trail.
3. **Identify the material change.** Match entities and typed events before using fuzzy text. Compare a filing’s changed guidance, identified product or affected facility with the previous version. Link a foreign event to a named exposure when the dates, geography and business mechanism fit. Group multiple affected names into one incident. Leave ambiguous identities and machine translations that change a negation, number or legal scope for review.
4. **Separate observations from possible consequences.** A watch records the mechanism, horizon, plausible adverse and benign paths, the best evidence against escalation, the next source that could distinguish them, and when to close. A credible single operator can establish its own outage. Several outlets repeating a rumor establish attention. A broad anomaly may trigger a search while supporting only a digest entry. Do not wait for a price move to open a justified watch. Record market reaction afterward as separate evidence.
5. **Choose delivery according to urgency.** Send a direct verified material change when waiting for the next review would cost preparation time. Send a conditional watch only when a credible observation connects to a documented exposure and an upcoming review or deadline. Bundle ordinary updates in the morning brief and reserve the discovery slot for weaker links. Use a single incident thread. Each distinct urgent development and any material correction to a prior push can reach the phone immediately, including overnight. No weekly cap delays them. Quiet hours apply to ordinary updates, with an explicit per-exposure mute available to the user.

**Severity, urgency and certainty answer different questions.** Ask how much of the investment thesis or shared exposure could change, what useful decision would be lost by waiting until the next brief, and what the source is in a position to know. A severe possible loss months away can be a daily watch. A direct issuer statement withdrawing a key forecast hours before a scheduled review warrants an immediate push. A statement that previously issued accounts can no longer be relied upon can itself create an urgent need for review. Judge magnitude against guidance, liquidity, recovery time, alternatives and shared exposure across names. A credible report of damage at a named sole-source plant just before an issuer call can also warrant a prompt *conditional* warning while customer impact is unresolved. An anonymous post about an entire country warrants verification. Comparing the cost of a missed preparation window with the cost of interrupting is preferable to assigning arbitrary weights to story volume. These dimensions are adapted from the Common Alerting Protocol and cost–loss research. The investor thresholds are proposals to test. [SEC Form 8-K, Item 4.02](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf) · [OASIS alert dimensions](https://docs.oasis-open.org/emergency/cap/v1.2/CAP-v1.2.html) · [ECMWF on the value of a forecast for a decision](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/media-resources/tim-palmer-festschrift/Evaluation-of-ensemble-forecasts)

For example, link a proposed trade restriction to a named product and an issuer’s next guidance date. The proposal earns a digest watch while its scope is unsettled. A published rule that includes the product and starts before that guidance date moves the question up the review list. Check for existing licenses and the issuer’s counterevidence. A denial affecting an identified shipment or a changed outlook justifies a new alert. If the final rule excludes the product, close the watch. The first warning can be useful well before either a shipment loss or a price movement is observable.

Each message should say **what changed, whose business it touches, what remains uncertain, and what to look for next**. For a calculation, show the inputs, date and unit. Use factual wording such as “the company reported,” interpretive wording such as “this exposes two names to the same supplier,” and conditional wording such as “if the line stays offline through Friday…”. Use probabilities only for defined outcomes with a time window, an appropriate base rate and a measurable track record. A language model can later suggest a missing relationship, translation or counterargument in a review queue. Require links to the exact evidence, and fall back to templates when a number, date or citation fails a check.

### Choosing the depth of investigation

Use the least expensive inquiry that could change the answer before it is needed. The following are trial limits, adjustable after observing actual latency, cost and corrections.

| When | Work and delivery | Stop or escalate when |
|---|---|---|
| A verified, time-sensitive fact arrives | Immediately send the changed fact, source and affected public names. Show reconciled position amounts, weights and scenarios only in the private local view. Use a template and the existing urgent-delivery target. | Defer a new model call, portfolio refresh or disputed causal chain. A delay or failure in research must still leave the recipient with the confirmed news. |
| There is a plausible connection the next brief can clarify | Compare the two primary passages, run a defined calculation, and check one competing explanation. Allow about five minutes of automated research per incident before the brief cutoff. | Send a follow-up only for a fact or implication that changes the earlier message. At timeout, identify the missing evidence and the next review time. Close incidents when the evidence reverses. |
| Two credible sources conflict, the potential consequences are material, or the investor asks a standalone question | Investigate on demand or at the weekly review. Record a bounded question, as-of time and decision deadline. Try the original-language filing, a direct operating source and a contrary explanation. Give one question about 15 minutes of human review as a pilot allowance. | Stop when the decision would be the same across the supported scenarios, the remaining unknown is undisclosed, or the time/spend allowance is reached. Reopen on a named observation. Material unresolved claims remain conditional. |

A model can propose an alias, retrieve a passage, translate text, or draft competing explanations. Keep the source value, unit, currency, period, reporting basis, publication version and calculation outside the prose. A material claim should link to a precise passage, table or filing item. An interpretation should identify supporting observations, counterevidence and what would change it. A forecast should add a horizon, resolution source and, where defensible, a probability. Validate calculations independently, inspect original-language terms that could reverse meaning, and propagate corrections to any brief or exposure map that relied on a superseded claim. A second model repeating a claim adds no independent evidence.

Benchmarks give different answers for different tasks. On 500 numerical lookups, FinRetrieval reported 90.8% accuracy for one model using a structured API and 19.8% with web search alone. The API and reference answers came from the same vendor, limiting transfer to an independent feed. Fin-RATE found more period, entity and comparison errors when moving from one filing to multiple periods or companies. On November 4, 2026 the highest-scoring configuration on Vals’ harder Finance Agent v2.1 benchmark received 67.85% under partial-credit grading at $8.91 and 33 minutes per test. None of these scores measures whether an alert is relevant to this investor. ## A prospective test that can reject the design

**Register the test before observing results.** Freeze the watchlist and dated relationships, source list, rule version, quiet hours, event definitions, horizon, matching tolerance, resolution source and last useful review time. For example, register material guidance changes for all pilot issuers, each calendar revision or release for selected BLS series, and issuer-confirmed production interruptions for named suppliers and facilities. An overnight warning might have an 08:00 ET decision deadline. An event beginning earlier can have negative lead to onset and positive lead to the review. Show both. A historical system such as EMBERS used time to a news report for one of its measures. That definition matters to anyone hoping to act before the underlying event. [EMBERS field study](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2016/papers/files/adf1132-muthiahA.pdf)

**Run two comparisons on the same calendar.** Baseline A is the actual broker/Quartr/IR alerts, a fixed premarket review and fixed calendar reminders, e.g. one business day ahead. Record actual arrival and first read where possible. Baseline B uses the same allowed inputs and polling as the custom service but sends simple name/form/keyword matches and the same reminders. A tests whether the work is worth owning. B isolates the value of connecting evidence from the value of having a faster source. Send shadow alerts through the real delivery path to a private test inbox. During review, present baseline and candidate messages without identifying the system where possible. Report the native settings as used and compare discretionary alerts at matched volume in the offline analysis. All qualifying urgent events remain eligible in both streams.

| Measure | How to score it |
|---|---|
| Timely awareness | For each matched fact, use receipt minus authoritative publication time, with p50/p90, counts and missing clocks by source. Break it into publication → first observed → decision → receipt to distinguish upstream lag from a local failure. Track time to first read separately. A shadow receipt measures potential delivery. |

### Testing the value of analysis separately

Pre-register two or three research questions per week during the same eight-week trial, recording each cutoff *before* the answer is known. Include calm days, routine releases, a mix of issuers and sources, and questions deliberately requiring an “unknown.” Draw a sample of incidents the system chose to skip. Use historical cases only as regression checks. A live head-to-head comparison asks whether the investor can get the answer faster using their usual research tools. A separate controlled comparison gives a reviewer with a spreadsheet, a deterministic template, and the optional model-assisted method **the same dated source packet**. That comparison isolates reasoning from differences in retrieval or entitlements.

|---|---|---|
| Reported fact or filing comparison | Before viewing candidate outputs, a reviewer records the material facts, qualifying language and source passages. Blind the answer labels where practical. For personalization, compare the **same incidents** under watchlist-only and size-aware explanations using hypothetical portfolios first. A material unsupported claim blocks that output and returns the affected method to review. ### Delivery tests and retention decisions

Before enabling live push, inject a burst of three distinct urgent events, 50 syndicated copies of one event, a retraction, and a restart with both live and expired warnings. Expect all three urgent events, one message for the duplicated incident, a prompt correction, and a recovery summary for expired items. Check the phone’s receipt time as well as the sender’s status.

**Decide after setup and at least eight frozen weeks**, then extend rare warning classes across reporting cycles. Suggested operating targets are ≥95% capture of eligible direct releases, p90 receipt within 15 minutes for predesignated immediate events, and routine digests delivered by 08:00 Eastern. Measure every urgent miss or delay. Aim for ≥75% of reviewed pushes rated worth the interruption. Review noise if more than two avoidable pushes occur in a week, while delivering all distinct qualifying urgent items. To retain the custom monitor, require two independently adjudicated cases with an extra useful review window over the native baseline, or at least 30 minutes/week less manual work at comparable coverage.

Set promotion thresholds per warning class in advance. For example, for a push predicting an issuer-confirmed interruption within 48 hours, require a resolved false-alarm ratio ≤25% and at least the same fraction of eligible incidents warned before the deadline as the baseline at comparable interruption volume, plus a useful lead-time gain. Keep it in shadow with fewer than ten resolved calls or too few eligible incidents to distinguish it from the baseline. Show raw paired wins, ties, losses and misses, with interval estimates for rates where meaningful. Two useful cases can justify continuing the trial. A claim of predictive skill requires more observations across conditions. Review every critical miss and suspend an affected rule after a material unsupported claim. Tune on completed periods, then start a new frozen period.

After the basic comparisons, a further experiment is a silent search for emerging themes across suppliers’ results, before the customer comments. Give a model only permitted, timestamped excerpts. Ask it to identify a recurring constraint, the named companies affected, a counterexample and the next announcement that could refute it. Compare its new, verified connections per hour of review and unsupported claims against a human keyword search on exactly the same corpus. This tests whether less structured analysis buys enough discovery to justify its cost and review burden.

If broker and issuer alerts tie on timeliness and the custom monitor saves little attention, keep the native alerts. A verified on-demand company brief or local portfolio worksheet may still earn its place. If only the alert wins, retain the minimum calculations it needs. If the audit keeps finding consequential foreign-language events outside the source list, trial a licensed global discovery product or an original-language specialist against those misses. If a particular playbook repeatedly buys a review window at low noise, expand that playbook to a second industry before adding a general-purpose forecasting agent.
